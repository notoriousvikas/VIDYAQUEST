Internal Cell Organelle NCERT Class 10

Chloroplasts (Green Plastids)

Green disc-shaped organelles suspended inside the cytoplasm of both kidney-shaped guard cells. Surrounding epidermal cells completely lack chloroplasts.

Physiological Function Inside Peel Traps sunlight to synthesize glucose during photosynthesis. This increases solute concentration inside guard cells, drawing water in by endosmosis to open the stomatal pore.
Board Exam Identification Clue Appears as distinct green granules inside guard cells even after pink-red Safranin staining.

NCERT Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Peel Epidermis: Fold a fresh Tradescantia or Rhoeo leaf and tear it obliquely to separate a thin, transparent lower epidermal peel.
  2. Keep Moist in Water: Immediately place the peel in a Petri dish containing water so it does not curl or dry out.
  3. Stain with Safranin: Add 2–3 drops of Safranin stain for ~2 minutes to colour cell walls and nuclei pink-red; rinse excess stain with water.
  4. Transfer to Slide: Place the stained peel flat at the centre of a clean glass slide using a brush/forceps.
  5. Mount in Glycerine: Put a drop of glycerine over the peel and gently lower a coverslip at a 45° angle using a mounting needle to avoid air bubbles.
  6. Observe: Blot excess glycerine with blotting paper and examine first under low power (10X), then high power (45X).

Essential Exam Precautions

  • Always peel from the lower surface of the leaf.
  • Never let the epidermal peel dry out; always mount in glycerine (not plain water, which evaporates quickly).
  • Lower the coverslip gently at 45° with a needle to prevent trapping air bubbles.
  • Avoid over-staining with safranin and wipe excess liquid with blotting paper.

🎓 NCERT Class 10 Viva-Voce Questions & Answers

Q1. Why is the epidermal peel taken from the lower surface of a leaf?

Ans: In dorsiventral (dicot) leaves, stomata are much more numerous on the lower (abaxial) surface to minimize direct sunlight exposure and prevent excessive water loss by transpiration.

Q2. Why are Tradescantia or Rhoeo leaves preferred for this experiment?

Ans: Their lower epidermis contains purple anthocyanin pigment and peels off easily as a single, uniform transparent cell layer, making the green guard cells stand out sharply.

Q3. What is the role of Safranin and Glycerine in preparing the temporary mount?

Ans: Safranin stains cell walls and nuclei pink-red for clear contrast. Glycerine is hygroscopic and prevents the delicate leaf peel from drying out (desiccation) during observation.

Q4. Why should the coverslip be lowered gently at a 45° angle using a mounting needle?

Ans: Lowering at 45° pushes air out gradually and prevents air bubbles from getting trapped, which otherwise appear as dark thick-walled circles blocking the view of stomata.

Q5. How do guard cells differ from surrounding epidermal cells?

Ans: Guard cells are kidney/bean-shaped, contain chloroplasts, and have differentially thickened walls (thick inelastic inner wall, thin elastic outer wall). Epidermal cells are irregular and lack chloroplasts.

Q6. How do guard cells regulate the opening and closing of the stomatal pore?

Ans: When water enters guard cells by endosmosis, they swell (become turgid) and their thin outer walls bulge outward, pulling the thick inner walls apart to open the pore. When they lose water (become flaccid), the pore closes.

Q7. State two main functions of stomata (NCERT Ch. 5: Life Processes).

Ans: (1) Massive exchange of gases (CO₂ intake for photosynthesis and O₂ release). (2) Loss of water vapour during transpiration, which creates transpiration pull.

Q8. Why should we not use the coarse adjustment knob under high power (45X)?

Ans: Under 45X, the working distance between the objective lens and slide is very small; turning the coarse knob can crack the coverslip and scratch the objective lens.
